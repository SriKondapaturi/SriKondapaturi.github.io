All-digital PLL on SkyWater SKY130

A phase-locked loop turns a slow, clean reference clock into a faster one that stays locked to it. An all-digital PLL replaces the analog charge pump and filter with logic, so the whole thing can be built from standard cells and pushed through a normal digital flow.

  • Designed the loop in synthesizable Verilog: counter-based time-to-digital phase detector, digital PI loop filter, standard-cell ring-oscillator DCO with a tap mux, programmable ÷N feedback, and an averaging lock detector.
  • Hardened through OpenLane/OpenROAD on SKY130 and closed timing at 40 MHz with sub-10% IR drop. DRC, LVS and antenna clean in Magic and KLayout; all 15 Tiny Tapeout prechecks passed on the first attempt.
  • Found and fixed an out-of-range DCO tap index that left half the taps undefined, so the ring could not oscillate at its operating point. Resized the chain from the tap count to 32 inverters and quantized the behavioural model to the same 16 taps.
  • Verified in cocotb with Icarus Verilog: locks at about 39.81 MHz for N = 4 from the 10 MHz reference, in roughly 650 µs of simulated time.

Multi-chiplet SoC with DVFS, CDC and hierarchical power management

A chiplet SoC splits one large system into smaller dies that talk over an on-chip network. It is how modern processors scale, and it makes physical design harder: each chiplet brings its own clocks, supplies and timing budget, and every crossing between them has to be made safe.

  • Owned the full Cadence Innovus flow on a 180K-gate SoC with 12 asynchronous clock domains: floorplanning, power planning, structured placement, customized CTS and routing.
  • Closed 513 MHz with multi-mode, multi-corner signoff in PrimeTime, scripting the timing and IR-drop ECOs in TCL across corners.
  • Reached 100% structural CDC/RDC closure in SpyGlass and JasperGold, with clean logic-equivalence signoff in Conformal.
  • Signed off physical verification clean in Calibre DRC/LVS and analysed IR drop and electromigration in RedHawk-SC.

8 × 8 systolic neural-network accelerator

A systolic array is a grid of multiply-accumulate cells that hand data to their neighbours every clock. It maps matrix multiplication, the core of neural-network inference, onto hardware with very little traffic back to memory. Google's TPU is built around one.

  • Wrote the RTL for 64 pipelined MAC cells (8-bit signed inputs, 32-bit accumulators) plus row-wise bias and ReLU, so the block outputs a finished neural-network layer. A self-checking ModelSim testbench matched every output to a golden model.
  • Split each MAC into separate multiply and accumulate stages. The critical path moved out of the array into the bias/ReLU output stage, and timing closed at 2.25 ns (444 MHz) in Design Compiler.
  • Took the 30,548-cell netlist through Innovus: 70% utilization floorplan, M9/M10 rings with an M7/M8 stripe mesh, CCOpt clock tree, NanoRoute, zero DRC violations, filler insertion.
  • Sized the power grid by hand before CTS: 24.05 mW after placement gives 25.3 mA through eleven parallel M8 stripes (≈ 1.03 Ω), so about 26 mV of IR drop against a 47.5 mV budget. No extra straps needed.

DFT, BIST and ATPG flow with Synopsys TetraMAX

Design-for-test adds hardware so a chip can be checked after it comes back from the fab. Scan chains let a tester shift a value into every flip-flop and read the result out; built-in self-test lets the chip generate and check its own patterns.

  • Integrated scan chains and BIST (LFSR/MISR) into an FSM controller through DFT-aware synthesis, then generated ATPG patterns in TetraMAX to reach 90.93% stuck-at fault coverage.
  • Stitched and balanced the scan chains with test-mode timing checked in both shift and capture, and validated the architecture across RTL and gate-level simulation.

03About

I finished my M.S. in Electrical and Computer Engineering at California State University, Fresno, where I spent two years as a research assistant to Prof. Aaron Stillmaker, chair of the ECE department, working on the physical implementation of multi-chiplet SoCs.

I am most at home between synthesis and signoff: the stretch where a netlist gets a floorplan, a power grid, a clock tree and wires, and then has to meet timing at every corner. I work in both the commercial stack industry signs off with and the open flow that let me take a chip to tapeout on my own. Along the way I helped about 25 graduate students through STA, SDC and CDC debugging.
