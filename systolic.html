8 × 8 systolic neural-network accelerator

GDSII · DRC cleanSole designer · 2025

A systolic array is a grid of multiply-accumulate cells that hand data to their neighbours every clock. It maps matrix multiplication, the core of neural-network inference, onto hardware with very little traffic back to memory. Google's TPU is built around one.

  • Wrote the RTL for 64 pipelined MAC cells (8-bit signed inputs, 32-bit accumulators) plus row-wise bias and ReLU, so the block outputs a finished neural-network layer. A self-checking ModelSim testbench matched every output to a golden model.
  • Split each MAC into separate multiply and accumulate stages. The critical path moved out of the array into the bias/ReLU output stage, and timing closed at 2.25 ns (444 MHz) in Design Compiler.
  • Took the 30,548-cell netlist through Innovus: 70% utilization floorplan, M9/M10 rings with an M7/M8 stripe mesh, CCOpt clock tree, NanoRoute, zero DRC violations, filler insertion.
  • Sized the power grid by hand before CTS: 24.05 mW after placement gives 25.3 mA through eleven parallel M8 stripes (≈ 1.03 Ω), so about 26 mV of IR drop against a 47.5 mV budget. No extra straps needed.
